Trust & Safety

Good connections.
Respectful beginnings.

Your comfort, choice, and privacy matter at every step. Take your time, stay curious, and keep your boundaries clear.

Our approach

Respect is part
of every introduction.

01

Choice comes first

An introduction is an invitation. Both people can say no, ask for time, or decide not to continue—without pressure or guilt.

02

Be clear about your role

If you are helping a family member, explain who manages the profile or conversation. Their voice and wishes should remain central.

03

Honesty matters

Represent yourself accurately. Do not impersonate someone, mislead people about your intentions, or share someone else’s photos without permission.

Pause when something feels wrong

You don’t owe anyone
your trust—or your money.

Requests for money, secrecy, urgent transfers, intimate images, or identity documents deserve caution. A convincing story or a familiar cultural background is not proof that a person is genuine.

At your pace

From first hello
to meeting in person.

Before an introduction+

Decide what you are comfortable sharing. Keep your home address, financial information, identity documents, passwords, and one-time codes private. Be clear about your intentions and any family involvement.

While getting to know someone+

Take time to notice consistency between what someone says and does. Do not send money to someone you have only met online. You can stop a conversation if there is pressure, harassment, or repeated disregard for your boundaries.

Before a first meeting+

Choose a public place, arrange your own transport, and tell someone you trust where you will be. Keep your phone available and make a plan to leave if you feel uncomfortable.

If you want to stop+

You do not need to justify a decision to step away. Use the blocking or reporting tools of the channel where the contact happens, if needed. Keep relevant messages if you want to raise a concern.

If you are supporting a family member+

Ask what kind of help they want and respect their pace. Family enthusiasm does not replace personal willingness. Do not share private photos or contact details without appropriate permission.

Immediate danger

Contact local emergency services and someone you trust. Favorite Hearts is not an emergency service.
